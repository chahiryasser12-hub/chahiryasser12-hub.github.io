Master Percentages: Discounts, Tips & Tax Made Easy

📅 July 20268 min readReviewed by Yasser Chahir

Percentages express a part per hundred. The key is identifying the base value before choosing the operation.

Start with the idea

To find p percent of x, calculate p ÷ 100 × x. To find what percentage a is of b, calculate a ÷ b × 100. Percentage change is (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

A percentage-point change is different from a percentage change. Moving from 10% to 12% is an increase of 2 percentage points, but a 20% relative increase.

Worked example

20% of 150 is 0.20 × 150 = 30. If a price rises from 80 to 100, the increase is 20 and the percentage increase is 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

Reversing a percentage is not symmetric. A 20% fall from 100 gives 80, but a 20% rise from 80 gives 96, not 100.

Common mistakes

  • Using the new value as the base for an increase
  • Confusing percentages with percentage points
  • Assuming equal increases and decreases cancel
  • Dividing by zero

Practical steps

  1. Identify the base or original value.
  2. Convert the percentage to a decimal.
  3. Apply the correct formula.
  4. Check whether the result is reasonable.
  5. State clearly whether the change is relative or in percentage points.
