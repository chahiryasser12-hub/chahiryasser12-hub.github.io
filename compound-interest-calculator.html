What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest earning interest. You deposit money, it earns a return, and then that return also starts earning returns. Over months and years this snowballs — slowly at first, then dramatically. It is the single most powerful force in personal finance, and understanding it can change how you save forever.

This calculator compounds monthly, which matches how most real-world savings accounts and investment platforms work. It also lets you add a regular monthly contribution, so you can model steady investing rather than a one-off deposit.

How to read your result

The big number is your future value — what your money could be worth at the end of the period. Below it, you can see your total contributions (what you actually put in) and interest earned (the growth on top). The gap between those two is compounding at work, and it widens fastest in the later years.

Why starting early beats starting big

Time matters more than the amount you invest. Someone who contributes a modest sum from age 25 often ends up with more than someone contributing double from age 40, because the early starter gives compounding many more years to work. The lesson is simple: the best time to start was years ago — the second best time is today.

Three things that boost your growth

  • Time — every extra year matters more than the last.
  • Rate of return — even one extra percent compounds hugely over decades.
  • Consistency — regular monthly contributions add fuel to the fire.

Remember that investment returns are never guaranteed. Use a conservative rate to keep your plan realistic.
