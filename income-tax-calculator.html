How the take-home pay calculator works

This calculator takes your gross annual income — your salary before any deductions — and applies your effective tax rate to estimate the tax you pay. It then shows your net income for the year and breaks it down into a monthly take-home figure so you can budget with confidence.

It is deliberately simple and country-agnostic: instead of hard-coding one country's tax brackets, it lets you enter the overall percentage you actually pay, which makes it useful anywhere in the world.

What is an effective tax rate?

Your effective tax rate is your total tax divided by your total income. It is almost always lower than your top marginal bracket, because lower portions of your income are taxed at lower rates. The easiest way to find yours is to look at a recent payslip or tax return: divide the total tax you paid by your gross income and multiply by 100.

Gross vs net income

Gross income is what you earn before tax and deductions. Net income — your take-home pay — is what remains after. When you are budgeting, planning a loan, or comparing job offers, the net figure is the one that matters, because it reflects the money you can actually spend and save.

Using the result to budget

  • Use your monthly take-home as the top of your budget.
  • Keep total housing costs under about a third of it.
  • Aim to save a slice of every paycheck automatically.
  • Remember this is an estimate — social contributions, pensions and local rules can change the exact number.
