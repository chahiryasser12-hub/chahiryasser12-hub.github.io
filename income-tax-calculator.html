Quick answer

This calculator gives an instant estimate from the values entered above. Use the result as a planning starting point, then review the assumptions and limitations.

How to use the calculator

Enter the requested values using consistent units. Change one input at a time when comparing scenarios, and verify every value before using the result.

Assumptions

The estimate uses the values currently entered and the formula described on this page. Any fees, taxes, timing rules or personal circumstances not requested are excluded.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing monthly and annual values
  • Using inconsistent measurement units
  • Changing several inputs at once when comparing scenarios
  • Treating an estimate as a quote, approval, diagnosis or guarantee

What this calculator does

This calculator converts gross income into an estimated net amount using the tax rate entered by the user.

Formula used

Estimated tax = gross income × effective tax rate. Estimated net income = gross income − estimated tax.

Worked example

With gross income of $50,000 and an effective rate of 20%, estimated tax is $10,000 and estimated net income is $40,000.

How to interpret the result

The result shows the effect of the selected effective rate, not a complete tax return.

Important limitations

It does not determine brackets, deductions, credits, social contributions, local taxes or filing status. Tax rules vary by country and year. Currency is a display unit and the result is not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is an effective tax rate?

+
It is total estimated tax divided by gross income, rather than the highest marginal bracket.

Does this include deductions and credits?

+
No. Enter a rate that already reflects your situation if you want a closer estimate.

Does it work for every country?

+
The arithmetic is generic, but tax rules differ by country, region and year.
