How the Navy body fat method works

The US Navy body fat formula estimates your body-fat percentage from your height and a couple of circumference measurements — neck and waist for men, with hip added for women. It requires nothing more than a soft tape measure, which is why it is one of the most popular at-home methods. This calculator applies the official Navy equations and adjusts automatically for men and women.

Reading your body fat category

Your result is shown as a percentage, alongside a fitness category. General ranges differ between men and women:

  • Essential — the minimum fat your body needs.
  • Athletic — typical of very fit, active people.
  • Fit — a lean, healthy range.
  • Average — common for the general population.
  • High — a level worth addressing with a professional.

Why body fat beats BMI for athletes

BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so a muscular person can look 'overweight' on BMI while being very lean. Body-fat percentage measures what actually matters for health and fitness — how much of your body is fat versus lean mass. That makes it a valuable companion to BMI, especially if you train regularly.

How to measure accurately

  • Use a soft tape, kept level and snug but not tight.
  • Measure the waist at its widest point, relaxed.
  • Measure the neck just below the larynx.
  • Take each measurement twice and average them for consistency.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the Navy method?

+
It is a reasonable estimate for most people, though not as precise as a DEXA scan or hydrostatic weighing.

What do I need to measure?

+
Height, neck and waist (plus hip for women), using a soft tape measure.

Is it different for men and women?

+
Yes — the formula and healthy ranges differ, and the calculator adjusts automatically.

Is my data stored?

+
No, it is calculated privately in your browser.
