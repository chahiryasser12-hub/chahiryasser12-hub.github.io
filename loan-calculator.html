How the loan calculator works

This calculator uses the standard amortizing-loan formula. Each month your payment covers the interest charged on the remaining balance, and the rest goes toward paying down the principal. Early on, more of your payment is interest; later, more goes to principal. The result is a single fixed monthly payment and a clear view of your total interest and total repaid.

It works for any fixed-rate installment loan — a personal loan, an auto loan, or a student loan. Change the amount, rate or term and every figure updates instantly so you can compare offers side by side.

Why total interest matters more than the monthly payment

Two loans can have the same monthly payment but wildly different total costs. A longer term lowers the monthly figure, which feels good — but it usually means you pay far more interest overall. Always look at the total interest and total repaid numbers, not just the monthly amount, before you sign anything.

APR vs interest rate

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. The APR (annual percentage rate) also rolls in certain fees, so it is usually a bit higher and is the better number for comparing lenders. This tool uses the interest rate; if a lender quotes only APR, you can enter that for a slightly more conservative estimate.

Ways to pay less interest

  • Choose the shortest term you can comfortably afford.
  • Make small extra payments toward the principal whenever you can.
  • Shop around for a lower rate, or refinance later if your credit improves.
  • Avoid rolling fees into the loan — pay them upfront if possible.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for a car loan?

+
Yes. It works for any fixed-rate installment loan — auto, personal or student.

Is my data saved anywhere?

+
No. Every calculation runs locally in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

What loan term should I choose?

+
The shortest one you can afford. Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but far less total interest.

Does it include fees?

+
No — it calculates principal and interest. Add origination fees separately, or enter the APR for a closer estimate.
